Patient simulators don’t act like humans… we fix it.

Prompted with a single question, current simulators dump the entire case information available to them in one overly cooperative turn. Our framework PatientsWithPersonality (PWP) assures that answers only refer to what is asked disclosing selectively across turns, the way real patients do.

Summary

Testing clinical LLMs under realistic conditions usually means slow, expensive user studies. Patient simulators promise a scalable alternative, but existing ones overshare information and miss the variability of real patient behavior, so benchmarks built on them overestimate clinical capability.

PatientsWithPersonality (PWP) parametrizes each simulated patient over HEXACO, the six-dimensional model psychologists use to describe personality, on top of a latent patient state. A behavioral role controls how the patient talks; a query-conditioned disclosure grid controls what it can reveal at each turn.

We evaluate PWP in the first direct comparison of patient simulators under a shared protocol, anchored in transcripts of real physician–patient encounters. Clinicians judge PWP conversations realistic at least as often as the recorded encounters themselves and far more often than prior simulators, and almost never flag them as “too informative”. PWP reproduces the disclosure pacing of real patients, its configured traits are recoverable, and on a downstream contextual-care benchmark it stays closest to the difficulty of the real visit where prior simulators make the task too easy.

Our Framework

Parametrizable Personality Space

Each simulated patient is parametrized over the six axes of the HEXACO model of personality, a CEFR language level, and a conversation type (spoken or written). Honesty-Humility and Conscientiousness gate what the patient discloses; Emotionality, eXtraversion and Openness shape how it communicates; Agreeableness sets its attitude towards the clinician.

H
Honesty-Humility
Truthful, downplayed, or denied disclosure of sensitive habits.
E
Emotionality
Emotional state driving demeanor across the conversation.
X
eXtraversion
Talkativeness and tendency toward conversational tangents.
A
Agreeableness
Cooperativeness with, or skepticism of, the clinician.
C
Conscientiousness
Precision of medical-history recall, unmasked by targeted questions.
O
Openness
Prior beliefs such as a fixed self-diagnosis.

Initializing the Patient State

A case description of personal background, lifestyle habits, and medical facts is combined with the parametrization into a behavioral role (tangent topics, emotional state, prior belief), synthesized by a latent-role prompt tuned with evolutionary prompt optimization, and a disclosure grid that holds every lifestyle and medical fact in personality-dependent variants.

Simulating Patient Responses

For each clinician turn, a meta-LLM classifies which case fields are requested and whether the turn is a yes/no question, an open question, or a statement. Only the requested fields enter the prompt, in the variant the latent state selects, and the conversational LLM generates the answer. Across the conversation, targeted follow-ups progressively unmask what the patient first recalled only vaguely.

Results

All simulators replay the physician turns of 30 real primary-care encounters from the US Department of Veterans Affairs, so every simulated answer can be compared with what the real patient said. Nine resident clinicians rated 540 blinded conversations.

Clinicians judged PWP realistic in 75.0% of conversations, ahead of the recorded encounters (46.7%), their LLM rephrasing (31.1%), PatientSim (30.0%) and AgentClinic (11.1%). PWP was flagged as too informative once in 180 conversations, against 24/90 for PatientSim and 56/90 for AgentClinic. Transcribed in-person speech loses overlapping and cut-off turns, which some clinicians read as broken. Without the one rater who assumed written chats, agreement rises (Gwet’s AC1 0.28 → 0.38) and every comparison stays significant. Leaving out in addition the columns rejected as inconsistent with prior context raises the recorded encounters to 66.1%, but PWP rises as well (87.4%).

PWP stays closest to the recorded patients on answer length and lexical diversity and is the only simulator that does not over-produce clinical jargon. It follows the disclosure pacing of the real patients, while PatientSim and AgentClinic front-load the case. The same holds on ACI-Bench, a broader cohort across specialties (77% female, ages 22–100).

Each HEXACO axis set to its extreme is recovered from the conversation by clinicians and by an LLM judge, closer to the configured level than the matching PatientSim persona. An independent judge (Claude Sonnet 5.5) agrees with the primary one (Spearman 0.91). Holding the case fixed, the seven PWP personas land further apart than PatientSim’s on 25 of 30 cases, because the latent state is generated per encounter rather than taken from a fixed template.

Downstream: Contextual-Care Benchmark

Many clinical problems have a non-medical cause: a patient’s diabetes is uncontrolled because they dislike pricking their finger. A plan that ignores it is a contextual error, medically correct but failing for this patient. The VHA encounters come with expert annotations of these causes. For 92 cases (206 coded causes), a doctor LLM interviews the simulated patient and states the cause and a plan for each problem. As reference, the same LLM reasons over the transcript of the real visit.

PatientSim and AgentClinic volunteer the cause, so every model makes significantly fewer contextual errors than on the real visit and the differences between models shrink towards the ceiling. PWP comes closest to the real visit, matching its error rate for Claude Sonnet 5.5, and keeps the gap between models intact. For both Gemini doctors PWP is still easier than the real visit (0.33 vs 0.45 and 0.23 vs 0.34). Making the persona withholding (H = C = 3) or emotional with a strong prior belief (E = O = 3) raises the difficulty further, through different mechanisms.

Outlook

PWP turns patient simulation into a controllable component of clinical LLM benchmarks: its default configuration matches the difficulty of real patients, and its personality axes make the same case harder in targeted ways, surfacing failure modes that uniformly cooperative simulators hide.

Next, we are matching the simulated population to the distribution of real patient personalities, moving beyond an ordinal mapping of the HEXACO axes, and extending the simulation beyond text towards the full patient encounter.
